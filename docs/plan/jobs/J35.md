## Goal
Every object in the bar is a real, separate thing: its own model at real
size, sitting on something real, carrying its own light and sound. You can
move any of them in the Studio, and later pick them up, knock them over or
use them (J24, J25).

## Scope

## Research (2026-10-26, the first corner; rule "Research, then build")
**What it touches:** the counter (`bar_counter_l`: `tools/assets/bar_counter_l.py`,
79 464 triangles, 29 MB of textures), the back bar (`back_bar_tall`:
`tools/assets/back_bar_tall.py`, 99 958 triangles, 17.5 MB), the stools
(`bar_stool`: `tools/assets/stools.py`, 5 632 triangles, 8 in the bar), the
pendant lamps over the counter (`pendant_lamp`), their kinds in
`assets/world/kinds/bar.json` (solids, the stool's seat at hip 0.9 m, the
counter's solids that the touch test taps), their places in
`assets/world/places/bar.json`, `assets/models/CREDITS.md`, `albedo.json` /
`rough_rules.json` (real brightness and roughness), `tools/assets/astc_pack.py`
(phone textures), the Studio's store (published after the deploy).
**Findings:**
- **Detail isn't what's missing:** the counter and back bar already have
  80k–100k triangles. What reads as fake (KI-45): sharp unbevelled edges,
  flat bottle labels, the bar top's step and rail gap, dashed shelf light
  strips, steel tops with seams and surfaces fighting at the same depth.
  Established practice for real-looking props (archviz, Unreal/Unity "hero"
  props): every hard edge bevelled a few millimetres (it catches the light),
  real-world scale, real scanned textures at an even sharpness (about 512–1024
  pixels a metre up close), roughness that varies across the surface, wear
  where hands go, contact shade (J91 now gives it), and nothing that floats.
- **Real sizes** (bar trade standards): bar top 1.05–1.10 m high (ours
  1.08), 0.50–0.60 m deep, overhang 0.20–0.25 m; stool seat 0.75–0.78 m;
  foot rail 0.18–0.23 m up; back bar counter about 0.90 m, shelves about
  0.35 m apart.
- **Poly Haven (CC0, reachable from here, 521 models):** `bar_chair_round_01`
  (a bar stool, seat height 0.75 m, 14 373 triangles, 1k textures 1.8 MB),
  `metal_stool_01` / `_03` (industrial), `hanging_industrial_lamp` (9 530),
  `industrial_pipe_lamp`, `industrial_caged_sconce`, `lightbulb_01`,
  `wine_bottles_01`, `wine_barrel_01`, `wooden_display_shelves_01`. No
  spirit bottles: ours stay scripted.
- **Bottles the way games do them:** a thin glass shell (the engine's
  see-through glass with brighter edges, `fs_model_blend`) and the drink as a
  separate solid mesh inside, so the colour reads without true refraction;
  labels as one shared picture with many labels on it, our own made-up
  brands (Charter: no real company's name or logo); copies of one bottle are
  drawn together (J87), so 200 bottles cost about as much as a few.
- **Blender** (needed to rebuild models): not installed in a fresh cloud
  machine; `tools/assets/get_blender.sh` downloads 4.5 (download.blender.org
  reachable, about 350 MB, a minute). Slimming Poly Haven models: Blender's
  Decimate keeps the UVs; textures at 1k, then ASTC for the phone.
- **Budget for the corner:** a stool at most 8k triangles (eight copies drawn
  together), the counter and the back bar at most about today's (80k and
  100k), corner textures at most 40 MB on the GPU (the whole bar is 246 MB
  today; the hall alone 92 MB, which J74 takes down).
- **Risks and earlier incidents:** the ASTC label rename broke textures live
  (incident 2026-10-26 astc-label-renamed: keep `image/x-looped-astc`);
  pictures compared area by area, not by average (square-of-light); the
  touch test taps the counter's solids (keep their ids and places); a new
  stool changes where you sit (the seat's hip height follows the model); the
  live world is the Studio's store (published after the deploy).
- **Order:** the kit first (Blender, the Poly Haven fetch-and-slim tool, the
  budget check), then the stools (smallest, proves the Poly Haven path), the
  counter, the back bar and its bottles, the pendant lamps, then your look on
  the phone.

## Design
- **The kit (J35):** one rule for every new thing: a kind in
  `assets/world/kinds/`, a model from a script in `tools/assets/` at real
  size, materials from `assets/world/materials.json`, and a budget (triangles
  and texture memory per thing, checked by a tool next to
  `tools/assets/glb_report.py`). Copies of one model are drawn together
  (instancing) so fifty bottles cost about as much as one. The inventory flags
  anything still baked into a room model, and that count goes down to zero.
- **Taking apart (J70–J73):** each room model in turn; every part becomes a
  thing where it stands today (same place, same look), so the bar looks the
  same before the remake's later steps improve it. Before/after pictures per
  piece.
- **Shells (J74):** what's left of each room model is walls, floors and
  ceilings, remade at real size with real materials; doors and windows become
  things.
- **Placement (J75):** every thing sits on something real; the Studio snaps
  to it; the place check refuses floating, sunk or overlapping things.
- **Light and sound (J76, J77):** each light and sound moves from its helper
  spot onto the thing that makes it.
- **Options not taken:** remaking everything in one go (too big for a
  session, and a broken bar in between); keeping room models and only adding
  new things (the user asked for every object to be real and separate).

## Pieces
Each job is split into pieces when it starts (one model or one group of
things per piece), each with its pictures and a phone check: "stand by the
fire: the sofa, the chair and the rug look as before; in the Studio, tap the
vase and move it".

## Done when
The inventory shows no room model holding objects; every thing sits on
something; every light and sound has its thing; the bar looks at least as
good on your phone, and the frame time is no worse (J37's report).

## Risks
- **Phone speed:** many separate things mean more draw calls. Instancing and
  the budget hold it; the frame time is measured before and after each step.
- **The live world:** the Studio's store is the live world; each step is
  published to the store after its deploy, as every world change is.
- **Looks:** Claude can't see the phone; your phone check judges each step.
