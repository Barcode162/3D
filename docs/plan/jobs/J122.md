**Chapter:** the engine
**Serves:** N2 (made from scratch), N3 (real light)
**From the user:** request `2026-11-02 #5` (really good, realistic lighting that costs as little as possible; the first shader looked amazing but went dark; the baked "one formula" feels fake; ray tracing was too expensive; a test world with villages of different peoples, different trees and zones, inside and outside, so the light is judged everywhere; "decode light into code"; combine the new techniques with the ones we had; research, and the Unreal study)
**Verdict:** needs an experiment: every piece below is known practice on its own; the mix that looks right on an iPhone in a browser is found by measuring each against our own path tracer (J116.1) and by the user's eye on the phone; several sessions

## What we know already
- The first renderer (the live bar): forward lamps, lamp shadow maps, bounced light from patches and probes on the GPU (WebGPU compute), light beams, eyes that adapt. It looked rich close to bulbs, but rooms went dark: no fill light (the reference showed a real room has much more), and the rule "light only from light sources" with nothing to carry bounce everywhere.
- The new engine (J116): everything baked into textures per light channel and relit by numbers. Cheap and on WebGL2 too, but frozen and flat: no light on moving things, no shading from the eye's side (reflections), so it reads as fake.
- Our path tracer (J116.1): the truth for any spot, minutes a picture. The measure for everything here.
- Unreal's study (J116): Lumen, MegaLights, DDGI and ReSTIR are built for big GPUs; their ideas, scaled down, are what a phone can take.

## The light, as code (the design)
One model for every place, inside and out, in real units (lux, lumens, candela):
1. **Surfaces** that react like real ones: base colour, roughness, metal (GGX), so wood, stone, water and metal differ.
2. **Sun and sky** from a time of day: the sun's colour through the air, a sky that matches it, the sky's light on everything.
3. **Shadows**: the sun's over hundreds of metres (cascades, soft edges); the nearest lamps' (cached while nothing moves).
4. **Bounce light**: a grid of probes; baked by our path tracer where the world holds still, updated a few rays a frame where it changes (Lumen's idea at phone size; WebGPU), baked only on WebGL2.
5. **Contact shade**: soft darkening where things meet (ambient occlusion).
6. **Eyes**: exposure that adapts like eyes, and a film-like curve: never pitch dark, never washed out.
7. **Air**: height fog lit by the sun and lamps.
Each piece has an off switch and a before/after.
