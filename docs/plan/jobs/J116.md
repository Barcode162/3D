## The revamp in one paragraph

Looped is rebuilt as a **skeleton of separate pieces**: the words (the
language), the world, the light, the drawing, physics. Each piece is
perfected in its own **lab** (a small test world with its own tests and a
page to try on the phone), and only then does **the bar inherit it all** in
one switch, when the user says. Everything in the world is **described in
words**: places, things, lights and what they do are sentences in Looped
Script, and every word is itself a small script made of other words. The
light is **one formula**: the room's light worked out once per light
channel and relit each frame by numbers the words compute, the same on
**WebGL2 and WebGPU**. Today's bar keeps running for players, frozen except
for fixes, until the switch.

## The language everywhere: a web of words

**Every word is a script.** A word's meaning is a few lines of Looped
Script, written in other words, down to a few dozen root words the engine
itself provides (the maths: `wave`, `noise`, `flicker`, `thump`; the
settings: colour, brightness, size; the stuff: a box, a model, a light). So
any word can be opened, read and changed: change `warm` and every warm thing
changes; a new word is a few lines. Precedents: [Inform 7](https://inform-7-handbook.readthedocs.io/en/latest/chapter_9_phrasing_&_punctuation/assertions/)
builds whole story worlds from English sentences ("The kitchen is a room");
[Attempto Controlled English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempto_Controlled_English)
is a subset of English in which every sentence has exactly one meaning
(a fixed set of sentence patterns). Ours does both, for a 3D world.

**Kinds of words**
- *Thing words* (`room`, `table`, `candle`, `lamp`): what it's made of (a
  model, boxes, a light), its settings, what it does by itself.
- *Describing words* (`warm`, `blue`, `dim`, `round`, `fast`, `gently`):
  change settings (`warm`: colour down 600 K), on things and on doings.
- *Doing words* (`flicker`, `glow`, `sway`, `breathe`, `pulse`): behaviour
  over time (the loop shapes; a light's channel weight in the formula).
- *Place words* (`on`, `in`, `by`, `over`, `under`, `against`, `in the
  middle of`, `in the corner of`): work out where a thing goes from the
  other thing's size (`on`: on top of it, centred).
- *Cause words* (`when`, `while`, `makes`, `until`, `after`): tie events to
  changes, as rules the runner keeps true ("while the fire is lit, the room
  is a little warmer").
- *Amount words*: numbers with units (`2 m`, `300 lumens`, `2700 K`,
  `8 s`, `40%`), `a little`, `very`, `twice`, `half`.

**The web.** Each word names the words it's made of (its script) and the
words it's *like* (`warm` is like fire, sunset, candle). The links give the
checker its hints ("`cosy` isn't a word yet; words like it: warm, soft,
dim"), a map of the dictionary to browse, and, later, suggestions while
writing.

**One sentence, one meaning.** A fixed set of sentence patterns; anything
else is an error with its line, column, plain words and a fix hint, never a
guess. Meanings are written down, not guessed while the bar runs (the
engine doesn't learn words by itself): Claude and the user write new words,
the checker proves them. Everything compiles to numbers once (settings, and
small budgeted programs over time), so a phone pays nothing for the words.

      a candle on the table

      made of the model candle.glb

      like: flame, fire, wax

**How the two drawing backends meet** (research): WebGPU groups resources
into bind groups and keeps textures and samplers apart; WebGL2 binds each
texture and uniform one by one with combined texture samplers
([W3C GPU group](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-gpu/2019Nov/0014.html),
[sokol's backends](https://sokol-d.dpldocs.dlang.org/sokol.gfx.EnvironmentDefaults.html)).
Our interface describes passes, pipelines and fixed binding slots; each
backend maps them its own way. The new scene shader is small (a few texture
reads per pixel), written once in WGSL and once in GLSL ES 3.0, with a test
that both give the same picture.

## The light: one formula

**The bar doesn't move; only its lights change.** Light transport is linear:
twice the lamp, twice every lit pixel, bounce and haze included. So
everything light does in this room can be worked out once, offline, per
*light channel* (a group of lamps that always change together), and each
frame the whole room is relit by a handful of numbers that scripts compute:

    pixel  = albedo · Σk  Uk(x) · wk(t)            diffuse: direct, shadows, every bounce, soft shade
           + Σi∈near4(x)  Vi(x) · GGX(n, v, li) · si(t)   highlights: four nearby lamps, baked visibility
           + Σk  Rk(dir) · wk(t)                    glossy reflections of the room
           + Σk  Hk(ray) · wk(t)                    the haze and the light beams
    w(t)   = M · s(t)          (M: k × C, worked out by the bake; k ≈ 4, C ≈ 12 channels)
    s(t)   = the light script  (each channel's brightness and colour now: flicker, dimmer, mood, music)
    exposure(t) = Σk wk(t) · Ek  (the eyes' brightness, known in advance per room: no GPU readback)

One linear operator, compiled from one script, sampled four times per
pixel. Today's 16 lamps a pixel, 40 shadow maps, a compute bounce, the soft
shade prepass, 24-step beams and the eye readback all collapse into a few
texture reads and a 4 × 12 matrix multiply on the CPU. Nothing in it needs
compute shaders, so **the same frame draws on WebGL2 and on WebGPU**.

## Research

**What the engine does now** (looped/, about 10,000 lines on WebGPU, 10 WGSL
shaders, 2,100 lines): forward lighting with a 48 × 48 floor grid of lamps
(up to 16 a pixel), 40 cube shadow maps, bounced light from CPU-traced
surfels and probes bounced on the GPU in a compute shader (looped/gi.*,
looped/radiosity.*: already "visibility once, light every frame", the seed of
this idea), a soft-shade prepass, light beams ray-marched per pixel with
shadow lookups, eye adaptation read back from the GPU, a mirror pass and a
reflection capture. Only the compute bounce has no WebGL2 form; the rest
ports, but it's all paid per pixel per frame, which is why the phone's GPU
runs flat out (reports SC7QKJ, GADATU: half the frames drew a picture on Ultra).

**Browsers** (2026): WebGPU ships in Chrome/Edge (desktop, Android 12+),
Safari 26 (iOS, macOS) and Firefox on Windows and Apple-silicon Macs; Firefox
on Linux and Android is still pre-release, and every phone not updated to
iOS 26 has none. WebGL2 runs in about every browser since 2021.
[WebGPU status wiki](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status),
[Wikipedia: WebGPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebGPU).

**The known methods, and why each alone is not the answer here**
- *Faster real-time forward lights* (what we tuned in J110–J111): still paid
  per pixel per frame; GI needs compute (no WebGL2).
- *Baked lightmaps + shadowmask* (Unity, Unreal): cheapest at run time, but
  frozen: our lamps flicker, dim, follow moods and music.
- *Enlighten-style precomputed radiosity* (Frostbite, Unity's old real-time
  GI): relightable bounce, but direct light and shadows still real-time, and
  the bounce solve runs every frame.
- *Precomputed radiance transfer* (Sloan 2002): relights from a moving sky,
  not from 60 local lamps.
- *DDGI, Lumen, ReSTIR, radiance cascades, neural caches*: built for scenes
  that move; heavy, need compute or ray tracing; overkill for a fixed room
  on a phone.

**The angle no one of these takes:** treat the room's whole light (direct,
shadows, all bounces, soft shade, reflections, haze) as **one linear operator
from channel brightnesses to pixels**, bake it per channel, compress it to
its few real degrees of freedom (a low-rank factorisation: the 12 channels
light the room in about 4 independent "shapes"), and let **the script
compiler choose the channels**: lamps driven by the same script expression
can never differ, so they bake as one; a channel whose bounce is too weak to
see gets no basis map. The language and the light become one system: the
script is both the light rig and the bake's specification. Pieces of this
exist separately; put together for a phone and a browser, it's ours.

**What it costs** (estimates to confirm by the bake):
- *Memory:* about 2,000 m² of surfaces at 4 cm a texel ≈ 1.3 M texels; 4
  basis maps, ASTC/ETC-compressed ≈ 3 MB; the visibility of the 4 nearest
  lamps (RGBA8) ≈ 1.3 MB compressed; 4 relightable reflection cubes (128²) and
  4 haze volumes (80 × 24 × 64) ≈ 2 MB. Under 10 MB for the room's light.
  Small props (glasses, bottles, chess pieces) and people read a probe grid
  (the same basis, as spherical harmonics) instead of their own maps.
- *GPU:* per pixel 4–6 texture reads, at most 4 highlight terms on glossy
  surfaces, 6–8 haze steps at quarter size. Expected 3–5× cheaper than today.
- *CPU:* the scripts and a 4 × 12 multiply a frame: microseconds.

**What moves still moves:** people, glasses, darts, chess pieces read the
probe grid; people cast soft capsule shadows (an analytic shape, no shadow
map); the piano's key glow and sparks stay emissive. A lamp moved in the
world means a re-bake (seconds to minutes, our own baker, in the build).

**The script language starts here, small and real** (J43's design, J78's
parser): the light rig is the first thing written in it.

    light strings: string-light-*          # one channel: they always move together
      colour 5000 K
      brightness 1

    light fire: light-fire
      colour 1700 K
      brightness 0.8 + 0.2 * flicker(time, 7)

    light pendants: light-bulb-1 … light-bulb-4
      when switch tapped: brightness to 0.2 over 1 second
      when switch tapped again: brightness to 1 over 1 second

    mood party
      strings colour 6500 K, brightness 1.3

Errors carry a line and a fix hint ("line 7: `fliker` isn't a function; did
you mean `flicker`?"). The checker also tells the baker which lamps are one
channel.

**Risks**
- Lightmap UVs for 167 models: generated by our Blender scripts at export
  (a second UV set); small props use probes instead.
- Seams and light leaking at map edges: the baker dilates charts and checks
  each texel's view of walls; the reference renderer shows any leak.
- Sharp shadows (stool legs on the floor) at 4 cm texels look softer than
  shadow maps: the near-4 visibility map is at 2 cm where shadows are sharp,
  and the reference comparison decides.
- The Studio (parked) loses live lamp moves until re-bake; scripts keep live
  brightness and colour.
- Naga (WGSL to GLSL) may not cover every shader: the new lighting shader is
  small, so it's written once and translated, or written twice by hand.

## Settle and repair (request 2026-10-27 #9)
The same idea for everything: **keep what's worked out, repair only what
changed.** Physics makes the world change (a stool knocked over, a glass
slid along the counter); a thing is *moving* while its body is awake (lit by
the probe grid, a soft shadow under it, highlights from the near lamps) and
becomes *part of the room* again once its body falls asleep (looped/physics.h
already puts resting bodies to sleep). Then only the light around it is
re-baked, in the background, a few milliseconds a frame:
- *What's dirty:* the surfaces and probes within the thing's reach of each
  lamp it can shadow (the lamp → thing → surface cone) plus a bounce radius
  around it; every other texel keeps its value.
- *Meanwhile:* the old light stays, the thing's soft shadow covers the
  difference, and the repaired texels fade in as they finish.
- *The same baker* (C++, runs natively for the full bake and in the browser
  for repairs, sliced into jobs), so a repair matches a full bake.
- *Beyond light, later:* the same "cache, invalidate locally, repair in the
  background" for the walkable floors, sound echo per room, and things'
  physics (furniture as bodies that can be pushed and knocked over).

## Found so far

- **J116.1 (the reference):** our path tracer (`looped/bake/light_paths.*`,
  `node tools/light.mjs ref`) renders the bar's 665,000 triangles with all
  63 lamps at their real size and every bounce (480 x 270, 512 paths a
  pixel, about 4.5 minutes a spot; the lab in about 30 s). With every
  bounce counted the room has much more fill light than today's renderer
  gives. A one-sided sign gets half its physical light in today's renderer
  (lumens / 2 pi, not / pi), and the world's lumens were tuned that way, so
  the baker keeps that rule until the lumens are re-measured. The reference
  uses each material's average colour, so a model whose glow is a texture
  (the pendant shade's bulb) glows all over in its pictures: cosmetic only.
  A branch-free box test made the ray tree twice as fast (also in the game).
- **J116.2 (Looped Script v0):** the bar's lights as script
  (`assets/world/rigs/bar.looped`, `looped/script/`): 10 channels; every
  lamp moves exactly as the world's loops move it (29,169 moments checked,
  difference 0); made words (`shape`) from request #10; 34 broken copies
  caught with their line and a fix hint; the bar's rig takes 300 of its
  8,000-step budget. It becomes the words piece's first lab test.
- **The unwrapper** (`looped/bake/unwrap.*`, `node tools/bake.mjs unwrap`):
  the bar's surfaces laid flat in one 2048 x 878 light texture (1.8 M
  texels at 5 cm; never-seen faces left out, high ones at 10 cm, fiddly
  models such as stools, plants and the piano lit from probes).

## Risks
- **A language that tries to understand everything:** one meaning per
  sentence, a fixed set of patterns; the language grows by adding words, not
  grammar. Each new pattern is its own small decision.
- **Two engines at once:** the old bar is frozen except for fixes while the
  skeleton grows; nothing is maintained twice for long.
- **The WebGL2 backend:** the new frame needs no compute shaders, so the
  scene shader stays small; written twice (WGSL, GLSL ES) with a same-picture
  test.
- **Converting the world:** the bar's kinds and places become words by a
  converter, with a test that the converted world equals the old one (as
  the rig's test does for its lights).
