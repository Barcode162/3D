# J116 · 4K Mania upgrade: smooth, exact, and a training pack

**Chapter:** the world
**Serves:** N3, N5
**From the user:** request `2026-10-28 #1` ("plan this out really well before we execute")
**Plan pictures:** the design canvas https://claude.ai/artifact/5L6D5whjC3oEf37C9sDvgo (the overview, why notes wobble, the play screen now and after, the finger test, the Training pack, the patterns, the 13 steps)
**Verdict:** doable now, with one experiment first. Our own code and our own
licensed songs. The tap freeze's cause is measured on the phone before it is
fixed (J116.1). 240 fps can't be shown: the iPhone 15 Pro Max screen tops out
at 120 Hz, and Safari draws 60 unless its "Prefer Page Rendering Updates near
60fps" flag is off. The closest thing that is possible: every tap judged at its
exact millisecond (what 240 fps buys on a computer), and the game light enough
to run at the full 120.

## Goal
4K Mania feels like a real rhythm game on the phone: notes glide at one even
speed, a tap never freezes the picture, four fingers work at once and hold
notes never drop on their own. The combo is smaller, with no circle behind it.
A **Training** pack of hand-built maps takes you step by step from about 2 to
6-7 stars, each stage training speed, chords and jacks, and reading and finger
control.

## Scope
The 4K Mania game (`bar/projects/mania/`), its song clock and touches on the
page (`bar/web/audio.js`, `bar/web/bridge.js`), the frame pacing while it plays
(`looped/gpu.*`, `bar/game.cpp`), new chart files for the 12 songs, a chart
tool, a star counter, the Handbook's 4K Mania pages and the rhythm test.
**Not in scope:** new songs (asked separately: adding a song is "ask first"),
the cabinet's look in the bar, multiplayer scores, the auto-made charts for
jukebox songs (they keep working and get the real star counter; better
auto-charts are a later idea).

## Research

### What the game does today (read in the code)
- **The song clock.** `trackTime()` (`bar/web/audio.js`) returns
  `ctx.currentTime - start - latency` straight from the audio context, read once
  per frame by `RhythmGame::update` (`songTime_`). The audio clock moves in
  jumps of its sound buffer, not smoothly, while frames come on the screen's
  beat (`frame(rafMs)` in `bar/game.cpp`). So a note's height moves by
  uneven steps from frame to frame: notes look like they speed up and slow
  down (the "SV" feel). Taps are judged against the same jumpy clock
  (`t = songTime_ - (nowMs - r.ms) / 1000`), so judgements wobble by the size
  of a jump too (MAX is only ±16 ms).
- **Touches.** Passive listeners on the canvas queue them with the event's own
  time (`bar/web/bridge.js`); the game reads them once a frame
  (`looped/input.cpp handleQueuedTouches`) and judges by that time, so the
  judgement itself doesn't wait for a frame. A finger's lane is fixed when it
  lands; a touch the phone cancels counts as still held (a hold note goes on).
  Sideways the screen is four tall zones; upright, the column under the finger.
- **What could freeze the picture on a tap** (none confirmed yet, so J116.1
  measures first): (1) iPhone's three-finger text gestures (a three-finger tap
  shows Undo / Copy / Paste, a three-finger swipe undoes): with three or more
  fingers down, iOS may hold the touches back while it decides, or cancel them,
  which would also explain dropped holds; (2) the sound unlock on every
  `touchend` (`audio.js unlock`) runs again whenever the audio context isn't
  "running" (iOS can leave it "interrupted"); (3) up to two frames queued on
  the GPU (`maxFramesInFlight = 2`, `looped/gpu.*`): a tap is seen up to two
  frames later (the GPU check already tries one: `bar/gpu_check.cpp
  oneQueued`, "a touch shows a frame sooner"); (4) the whole bar's logic
  (avatars, room, physics, sounds) still runs every frame under the game,
  although only the text layer is drawn.
- **Frame rate.** The page draws once per `requestAnimationFrame`; Safari on
  iPhone gives 60 a second by default, 120 on ProMotion screens when the
  user turns off Settings → Apps → Safari → Advanced → Feature Flags →
  "Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps" (iOS 26 path, per heise and
  iphone-ticker). Nothing a page can do changes that.
- **Charts.** Every song's chart is made on the phone by listening
  (`rhythm_chart.cpp`: onsets in four bands, tempo, notes snapped to the beat,
  lanes by pitch, chords on loud hits, holds on ringing sounds). That gives
  music-following but random patterns: no pattern is ever chosen on purpose,
  so maps can't train anything and can't be consistent from stage to stage.
- **Stars.** A guess from notes per second, chords and holds
  (`RhythmGame::update`, kAnalyzing): not osu!'s scale.
- **The combo.** `drawPlay`: text `min(column × 0.75, 44 px)` tall, popping
  22 % on each hit, over a disc 1.3× its size at 12 % opacity.
- **Everything that uses what will change:** `bar/projects/mania/rhythm.*`,
  `rhythm_chart.*`; `bar/web/audio.js` (track load/play/time, preview, unlock);
  `bar/web/bridge.js` (touch queue); `looped/input.cpp` (presses, capture);
  `looped/gpu.*` (frames in flight); `bar/game.cpp` (the frame, the hidden bar
  under the game, `?rhythm=` / `?autoplay`); `bar/test_hooks.*`;
  `bar/hud.cpp` (draws the game; the cabinet screen from `screenState`);
  `tools/rhythm_test.mjs` and the `rhythm` check; the phone's remembered
  `rhSpeed`, `rhSkin`, `rhAcc.*`, `rhScore.*`, `rhStars.*` (a song's best stays
  under its title and difficulty; the pack's maps get their own keys);
  `docs/handbook/systems/world/4k-mania.md`; `assets/music/rhythm/` and
  `assets/music/CREDITS.md`.

### How established games do it
- **One smooth clock.** osu! (lazer) drives gameplay from an interpolating
  clock: between the audio's updates it runs on a precise timer and only eases
  toward the audio when they drift apart (osu! release notes 2023.803.0). The
  page's equivalent: map the audio clock onto `performance.now()` once, run
  the song time from it, and correct drift gently; `getOutputTimestamp()` is
  the standard pairing but Safari's has returned wrong values (WebKit bug
  264247, Apple forum 696356), so it's used only if it passes a sanity check.
- **Input at the touch's own time.** osu! reads input at 1000 Hz and judges by
  timestamps, so the frame rate doesn't touch accuracy. Ours already timestamps
  touches; with one clock both the picture and the judgement use the same time.
- **Draw for when the frame is seen**, not when it's started: the note
  positions are computed for the frame's display time.
- **An offset setting** (osu!'s "universal offset"): a tap-along to set the
  phone's and headphones' delay.

### Mapping: what works and how (osu! wiki, ranking criteria, Etterna wiki)
- **Pattern words** (osu! wiki pattern pages; Etterna "Common Pattern
  Terminology"): jump (2 at once), hand (3), quad (4); stream (constant run on
  one snap), roll (1234), split roll (1243 / 4132), stairs; jumpstream (a 1/4
  stream with jumps), handstream (with hands), quadstream; trill, jumptrill
  ([12][34]), split jumptrill ([13][24] harder, [14][23] easier); jack (same
  column), minijack (two), longjack (5-6+), chordjack, gluts (jumpjacks
  shifting columns); anchor (one column busy inside a stream), runningman (an
  anchored stream, the anchor off the other notes); burst; polyrhythm (e.g.
  1/4 against 1/3); graces / flams (notes a hair apart); holds: shield (a note
  right before a hold in its column), reverse shield (right after), inverse
  (every gap filled with holds: a reading test), holds on one hand while the
  other taps (hand independence).
- **What trains what:** speed and stamina = streams and long jumpstreams;
  chords and timing under load = jumpstream, handstream, chordjack; jack speed
  = minijacks, longjacks, gluts; finger control = trills, split jumptrills,
  rolls, anchors, runningmen, holds against taps; reading = density changes,
  1/3 and 1/6 rhythms, polyrhythms, graces, inverse and mixed hold lengths.
  Dan courses (Reform 4K) test each level with four maps: jack, tech, speed,
  stamina; the "Celestial" course clears at 97 %.
- **Rules per level** (osu!mania ranking criteria, written for ~180 BPM and
  scaled by BPM, 120 and 240 being the breaking points): Easy: mostly 1/1, no
  consecutive 1/4, at most 5 consecutive 1/2, chords of 2, holds ≥ 1 beat.
  Normal: no consecutive 1/6, at most 5 consecutive 1/4, no chords inside 1/4
  runs, no 1/4 jacks or shields, anchors under 3, chords of 2 (4K), holds ≥ 1/2
  beat. Hard: no consecutive 1/8, a 1/2 pause after each long 1/4 phrase, chords
  in 1/4 streams ≥ 1 beat apart, anchors under 5, trills ≤ 9 notes, 1/4 jacks
  only with rest, chords of 3 (4K), holds ≥ 1/4 beat. Insane: no split rolls or
  complicated 1/8 longer than 4 beats, no 1/6+ hold streams, no chords inside
  1/8 runs, split jumptrills ≤ 9 notes. Every level: no unjustified spikes;
  auto must reach a full score; no empty column. General: map what you hear,
  the same sound gets the same pattern, chords on heavy hits, the densest part
  where the music is loudest, balance the hands.
- **Stars.** osu!mania's star rating (ppy/osu, `Difficulty/Skills/Strain.cs`,
  version 20241007, MIT licence): for each note, a column strain (decays to
  12.5 % a second, +2 per note, ×1.25 under a hold) and an overall strain
  (decays to 30 % a second, +1 per note, more for awkward releases); the
  hardest of each 400 ms section, sorted, summed with weights 0.9ⁿ, × 0.018.
  Re-made here as a prototype and priced on steady patterns: 1/2 singles at
  150 BPM ≈ 1.5★; 1/4 stream at 150 ≈ 2.6★; jumpstream (a jump every half
  beat) at 160 ≈ 4.0★; jumptrill at 160 ≈ 4.5★; dense handstream at 160 ≈
  5.2★; 1/4 chordjack at 150 ≈ 5.6★; dense handstream at 200 ≈ 6.3★. A whole
  map with rests rates a little under its hardest pattern.
- **Our 12 songs** (all Kevin MacLeod, CC BY 4.0, measured here by their kick
  drums): Electro Cabello ~117 BPM 3:11, Galactic Rap 120 2:22, Voxel
  Revolution 122 2:10, Overworld 133 2:53, Monkeys Spinning Monkeys 144 2:05,
  Pixel Peeker Polka 145 3:22, Run Amok 148 1:47, Blip Stream 150 4:45, Neon
  Laser Horizon 160 2:59, Hyperfun ~200 3:53, Funkorama ~202 3:21, Itty Bitty
  8 Bit ~218 3:14 (the last three may be counted at half speed; J116.8 settles
  it). 2-5.5★ fits the 117-160 songs; 6-7★ needs the ~200 BPM songs or a
  couple of faster ones.

### Risks and earlier lessons
- **Claude can't hear the songs** (Charter §4): maps follow what the analysis
  finds (beats, drums, held sounds); the user is the ear, batch by batch.
- **A fix for the wrong cause**: so J116.1 measures on the phone first and the
  fixes follow the report (rule "Research, then build"; the lighting job J113
  needed several rounds because causes were guessed).
- **Touch handling is shared** with the piano and the racing pedals: changes
  stay inside the game's capture mode, and a listener change (passive or not)
  is tried behind a switch first (bridge.js chose passive because waiting
  listeners once cost a frame per tap).
- **Stars may shift** for charts already played: bests stay; only the star
  number changes.

## Design
- **The clock** (J116.2): `audio.js` keeps a song clock anchored to
  `performance.now()` at the track's start, corrected toward the audio clock
  by a small fraction each frame (a big jump, over 50 ms, snaps); one function
  gives song time for any page time. The game draws notes for the frame's
  display time and judges a tap at its touch time through the same function.
- **The freeze** (J116.1 → J116.3): the test screen shows every finger, counts
  cancelled and late touches, frame times and the clock's steps; its numbers go
  into the phone report. Fixes follow what it shows, from the list above.
- **Frames** (J116.4): while playing, one frame queued on the GPU and only the
  game's own work (the room connection keeps going); a frame under 4 ms of work
  so 120 Hz holds.
- **Charts as files** (J116.7): `assets/music/rhythm/charts/<map>.json`
  (song, stage, skill, tempo, offset, notes: time, lane, end), written by a chart
  tool from a short map script per map (sections by bar, each with its pattern
  and its music cues); the tool checks the stage's rules (above) and the star
  target, and refuses a map that breaks them. One star counter in C++ for the
  game and the tool (built natively for the tool), so they never disagree.
- **The pack** (J116.9-13): 10 stages, about +0.5★ each (2.0, 2.5 … 6.5-7.0),
  three maps a stage: **Speed** (streams → jumpstream → handstream, stamina in
  the later stages), **Chords & jacks** (jumps → minijacks → chordjacks → dense
  chordjack), **Tech** (reading and finger control: trills, rolls, split
  jumptrills, anchors, runningmen, triplets and polyrhythms, holds against
  taps, shields, inverse). The three maps of a stage sit within 0.15★ of each
  other. No scroll-speed changes (SV) anywhere. A map is cleared at 95 %
  accuracy; a stage when its three are. Stages 1-2 with the ~120 BPM songs,
  climbing with tempo; Blip Stream (4:45) is the stamina map.
- **Options not taken:** improving the auto-chart maker instead (it can't
  choose patterns on purpose, so no consistency); writing every note by hand
  (thousands of notes per map, no checks); showing 240 fps (the screen can't).

## Risks
- The freeze has a cause no page can change (an iOS gesture): then the test
  shows it, the closest fix is chosen (e.g. a setting to turn off, or a play
  position that avoids it) and the user decides.
- 6.5-7★ with musical maps may need faster songs than ours: then one question
  to the user (adding licensed songs is "ask first").
- Maps that read well on paper may feel wrong in the hand: each batch waits for
  the user's play before the next one builds on it.
