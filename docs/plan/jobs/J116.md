## The single best answer

**The bar doesn't move; only its lights change.** Light transport is linear:
twice the lamp, twice every lit pixel, bounce and haze included. So
everything light does in this room can be worked out once, offline, per
*light channel* (a group of lamps that always change together), and each
frame the whole room is relit by a handful of numbers that scripts compute:

    pixel  = albedo · Σk  Uk(x) · wk(t)            diffuse: direct, shadows, every bounce, soft shade
           + Σi∈near4(x)  Vi(x) · GGX(n, v, li) · si(t)   highlights: four nearby lamps, baked visibility
           + Σk  Rk(dir) · wk(t)                    glossy reflections of the room
           + Σk  Hk(ray) · wk(t)                    the haze and the light beams
    w(t)   = M · s(t)          (M: k × C, worked out by the bake; k ≈ 4, C ≈ 12 channels)
    s(t)   = the light script  (each channel's brightness and colour now: flicker, dimmer, mood, music)
    exposure(t) = Σk wk(t) · Ek  (the eyes' brightness, known in advance per room: no GPU readback)

One linear operator, compiled from one script, sampled four times per
pixel. Today's 16 lamps a pixel, 40 shadow maps, a compute bounce, the soft
shade prepass, 24-step beams and the eye readback all collapse into a few
texture reads and a 4 × 12 matrix multiply on the CPU. Nothing in it needs
compute shaders, so **the same frame draws on WebGL2 and on WebGPU**.

## Research

**What the engine does now** (looped/, about 10,000 lines on WebGPU, 10 WGSL
shaders, 2,100 lines): forward lighting with a 48 × 48 floor grid of lamps
(up to 16 a pixel), 40 cube shadow maps, bounced light from CPU-traced
surfels and probes bounced on the GPU in a compute shader (looped/gi.*,
looped/radiosity.*: already "visibility once, light every frame", the seed of
this idea), a soft-shade prepass, light beams ray-marched per pixel with
shadow lookups, eye adaptation read back from the GPU, a mirror pass and a
reflection capture. Only the compute bounce has no WebGL2 form; the rest
ports, but it's all paid per pixel per frame, which is why the phone's GPU
runs flat out (reports SC7QKJ, GADATU: half the frames drew a picture on Ultra).

**Browsers** (2026): WebGPU ships in Chrome/Edge (desktop, Android 12+),
Safari 26 (iOS, macOS) and Firefox on Windows and Apple-silicon Macs; Firefox
on Linux and Android is still pre-release, and every phone not updated to
iOS 26 has none. WebGL2 runs in about every browser since 2021.
[WebGPU status wiki](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status),
[Wikipedia: WebGPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebGPU).

**The known methods, and why each alone is not the answer here**
- *Faster real-time forward lights* (what we tuned in J110–J111): still paid
  per pixel per frame; GI needs compute (no WebGL2).
- *Baked lightmaps + shadowmask* (Unity, Unreal): cheapest at run time, but
  frozen: our lamps flicker, dim, follow moods and music.
- *Enlighten-style precomputed radiosity* (Frostbite, Unity's old real-time
  GI): relightable bounce, but direct light and shadows still real-time, and
  the bounce solve runs every frame.
- *Precomputed radiance transfer* (Sloan 2002): relights from a moving sky,
  not from 60 local lamps.
- *DDGI, Lumen, ReSTIR, radiance cascades, neural caches*: built for scenes
  that move; heavy, need compute or ray tracing; overkill for a fixed room
  on a phone.

**The angle no one of these takes:** treat the room's whole light (direct,
shadows, all bounces, soft shade, reflections, haze) as **one linear operator
from channel brightnesses to pixels**, bake it per channel, compress it to
its few real degrees of freedom (a low-rank factorisation: the 12 channels
light the room in about 4 independent "shapes"), and let **the script
compiler choose the channels**: lamps driven by the same script expression
can never differ, so they bake as one; a channel whose bounce is too weak to
see gets no basis map. The language and the light become one system: the
script is both the light rig and the bake's specification. Pieces of this
exist separately; put together for a phone and a browser, it's ours.

**What it costs** (estimates to confirm by the bake):
- *Memory:* about 2,000 m² of surfaces at 4 cm a texel ≈ 1.3 M texels; 4
  basis maps, ASTC/ETC-compressed ≈ 3 MB; the visibility of the 4 nearest
  lamps (RGBA8) ≈ 1.3 MB compressed; 4 relightable reflection cubes (128²) and
  4 haze volumes (80 × 24 × 64) ≈ 2 MB. Under 10 MB for the room's light.
  Small props (glasses, bottles, chess pieces) and people read a probe grid
  (the same basis, as spherical harmonics) instead of their own maps.
- *GPU:* per pixel 4–6 texture reads, at most 4 highlight terms on glossy
  surfaces, 6–8 haze steps at quarter size. Expected 3–5× cheaper than today.
- *CPU:* the scripts and a 4 × 12 multiply a frame: microseconds.

**What moves still moves:** people, glasses, darts, chess pieces read the
probe grid; people cast soft capsule shadows (an analytic shape, no shadow
map); the piano's key glow and sparks stay emissive. A lamp moved in the
world means a re-bake (seconds to minutes, our own baker, in the build).

**The script language starts here, small and real** (J43's design, J78's
parser): the light rig is the first thing written in it.

    light strings: string-light-*          # one channel: they always move together
      colour 5000 K
      brightness 1

    light fire: light-fire
      colour 1700 K
      brightness 0.8 + 0.2 * flicker(time, 7)

    light pendants: light-bulb-1 … light-bulb-4
      when switch tapped: brightness to 0.2 over 1 second
      when switch tapped again: brightness to 1 over 1 second

    mood party
      strings colour 6500 K, brightness 1.3

Errors carry a line and a fix hint ("line 7: `fliker` isn't a function; did
you mean `flicker`?"). The checker also tells the baker which lamps are one
channel.

**Risks**
- Lightmap UVs for 167 models: generated by our Blender scripts at export
  (a second UV set); small props use probes instead.
- Seams and light leaking at map edges: the baker dilates charts and checks
  each texel's view of walls; the reference renderer shows any leak.
- Sharp shadows (stool legs on the floor) at 4 cm texels look softer than
  shadow maps: the near-4 visibility map is at 2 cm where shadows are sharp,
  and the reference comparison decides.
- The Studio (parked) loses live lamp moves until re-bake; scripts keep live
  brightness and colour.
- Naga (WGSL to GLSL) may not cover every shader: the new lighting shader is
  small, so it's written once and translated, or written twice by hand.

## Pieces (one batch, one ship; the old lighting stays until the user says so)
- **J116.1** The reference: our own path tracer (looped/bvh, the radiosity
  code) renders the bar at five spots with every light correct, as the
  standard every step is measured against (error and time numbers). *Check:*
  the pictures, side by side with today's live bar.
- **J116.2** Looped Script v0: lexer, parser, checker (line, column, plain
  words, fix hint), a tiny safe evaluator (step budget), for light rigs; every
  light loop in the world's kinds written as script; the checker's channels.
  Native tests on the examples and on broken copies. *Check:* the rig file
  reads like the example above.
- **J116.3** The baker: lightmap UVs at export; per-channel transport
  (direct, shadows, bounces), the low-rank basis, near-4 visibility, the
  probe grid, reflection and haze bases, the exposure table. *Check:* baked
  pictures match the reference within a set error.
- **J116.4** The formula at run time on WebGPU behind `?light=formula`,
  driven by the script; phone report against today's numbers. *Check:* same
  spots, same look, the GPU check's frames all drawn.
- **J116.5** The render layer split in two backends, WebGL2 (Emscripten's
  GLES3) beside WebGPU, chosen by the browser; the scene, text, pictures,
  mirror and post on both; the race after. A clear page for anything that has
  neither. *Check:* Firefox on Android, an older iPhone.
- **J116.6** Tune and decide: quality levels become map resolutions and
  haze steps; the old real-time lighting removed only when the user says.

## Done when
The bar draws on WebGL2 and WebGPU with one lighting formula driven by a
script, matching the reference within the set error, every frame drawn on
the user's phone on Ultra, and the user says it looks right.
